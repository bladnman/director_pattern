Why the Director ran everything at full strength, and stopped being a steward

One Project Director session in this source repo, 21:10–01:05 UTC. It was diagnosed from the session's own records, then revised twice by independent review. The corrections are part of the story.

Diagnosis: revised by review Some fixes published in starter 3.1 (2d4ce6c) Live proof: not yet

The short answer

Two failures with one shape: it followed the letter and skipped the judgment

Settings

9 of the first 12 workers were sent with no tier and no effort, so they inherited the Director's strongest tier at extra-high. They included a lookup-only inventory and two mechanical edits.

They used 74% of all subagent output. The record said "inherited, not set", and that passed for a choice.

Stewardship

A request to roll changes out to the usual projects became nine outside workers editing other projects' installations. That bypassed the existing route, where each project's own Director updates itself.

When the owner suggested that route mid-flight, the Director recommended letting its own workers finish.

What happened

The session, around one idle compaction

Five workers inherited before the compaction, while the staffing text was in context. So compaction explains the later behaviour, not the whole settings failure.

What happened · the rollout

The literal request became the plan

  1. The owner asks for the changes to be rolled out to the usual projects, in parallel.
  2. In the same response, the plan already says "dispatch parallel project updates". The update procedure is read only to line 120 of 363.
  3. Nine source-repo workers are dispatched to edit other projects' installations.
  4. The owner's suggestion arrives after the dispatches: start a session in each project and let its own Director update itself. That is the existing route.
  5. The Director's recommended option: "finish, then sessions verify". The owner accepts the recommendation.
  6. After the owner objects strongly, the remaining workers are stopped. Six other projects had already been patched from outside. Each worker followed its target's own steps; no one asked who should act.

Why · request recording after compaction

After compaction, records are made by imitation, not by protocol

Copying earlier commands

43–100% of record commands per segment match command shapes used earlier in the session. Five segments searched their own history for old commands to reuse. The record-keeping manuals were rarely re-read, and errors clustered on shapes the agent hadn't used before (a human gate, a revise, a link).

What held up

The helper's required flags force imitation into a valid form, which is why the stores look right. Recorder subagents that loaded their manual before every write, on the older layout, were not affected by compaction.

factValidation protects the form of the records, not the judgment about what to record and how to split it.

hypothesisThe flat starter makes narrow record operations "ordinary Director duties, not a reason to staff a Recorder" (R18). That moved record work onto the Director's memory, which is exactly what compaction erases.

open Two fix directions, both to be decided against R18's cost reasoning

(1) A light record-keeping roster role whose executor loads a short manual every time. It would be the default for any record write the Director hasn't just done, or the route after a compaction. (2) Keep record keeping with the Director, plus the re-load rule and a "capture before work" line in the AGENTS.md instruction block.

Why · revised root cause

Stance first, compaction second, the partial read last

Primary

No steward stance

Nothing told it to question the literal request, its own plan or its own recommendation. It chose the route before reading the procedure, and the authority guard went unapplied while in context. Host per-turn defaults filled the gap: do exactly that · don't spawn · effort only when asked.

What the first diagnosis got wrong · review 1

The first review corrected the settings diagnosis

An independent read-only reviewer, run on the strongest tier at medium effort with both set explicitly, tried to falsify it. The table, the shares, and the finding that the instructions were loaded all held.

Corrected

The token counts were doubled, counted once per content block. After recounting once per message, the shares were unchanged.

Demoted to a hypothesis

"The new strong default made it worse." No dispatch ran under that text installed, and when it was cited, it was read as calling for a lighter tier.

A missing cause was added

The user-level effortLevel: xhigh is where extra-high came from. The local guard's "session preferences remain" can read as "keep it".

The fix was strengthened

"Set both" alone fails the host's test, which wants a specific level. The default now has to name one: high.

What the first diagnosis got wrong · review 2

The second review moved the headline

An independent reviewer, run on the strongest tier at high effort, reviewed the rollout and compaction diagnosis.

  • The project file was not lost. The host re-attached it, so the cross-project authority guard was in context and still was not applied. The compaction was an idle one.
  • The route was chosen before the procedure was read. The read was confirmatory: it stopped once nothing visibly contradicted the plan. Compaction removed no knowledge of UPDATES, because UPDATES had never been read.
  • A host posture was missing: "do exactly that", present on every turn for spawned sessions.
  • The reuse and staffer clauses were demoted to design flaws, because they had themselves been compacted away before the rollout.
  • Six other projects were patched from outside, not seven. One worker was updating this repo's own install.
The Director's own blocking correction of the wording

A "re-read after compaction" rule placed only in START and DIRECTOR is circular, because those are the files compaction removes. The trigger must live in the AGENTS.md instruction block, generated by pattern/starter/install.py.

A correction from the owner · 2026-11-08

S3 retracted: the Director reads a roster, not other roles' manuals

Retracted

S3 (the Director fully reads any procedure whose route it decides) is withdrawn as pollution by instruction. Other roles' instructions linger in the Director and pull against its job. It also defeats the structure: a Director holds context and routes work. The review's "scoped form" is superseded too.

Roster

A summary of every role, other Directors included: what it does, when to pick it, a suggested tier and effort, and its ownership facts. A general worker entry is mandatory. Only executors read a role's own instructions.

Bail points

Direct for tiny, quick work, such as starting the Tinka server. A general worker for tiny but slow work. A sub-director for complex work. There is no fixed two-tier structure.

Sub-director

It owns one request, or a carved-out part of one. It writes its own sub-request, staffs workers, reconciles the result, and reports progress and completion.

proposal, not yet reviewed Draft complexity test

Use a sub-director for (a) several parts to reconcile, (b) several rounds or outside waits, (c) heavy evaluation, (d) its own progress reports, or (e) its own gates. If you would have to supervise it, give it a supervisor. Otherwise a worker for one bounded pass, or act directly for a quick action.

How this would have been prevented

One roster-level fact, not a full read of the manual

Each installation's update is owned by that installation's own Director. To update another project, ask its Director.

This fact sits in the roster entry for installation updates, or for another project's Director. Knowing who owns the work is enough to route it; how the work is done stays with the owner.

The rollout

The ownership fact routes it to one session per project, or to a 🖐️ user action. No outside workers, and no need to read UPDATES in order to decide.

The settings

Each roster entry suggests a tier and effort, and the Director sets both explicitly, with a reason. A lookup inventory gets a light suggestion.

The audit itself

The audit ran dozens of steps inline, each "a handful of tool calls", until the owner asked why it was not delegating. It had parts and its own reviewers, so the complexity test makes it one sub-director.

The audit's lapse was the same shape again: always-present host text ("when in doubt, don't spawn") won over a pattern rule read once. What stands with this: the steward stance, re-load in the AGENTS.md instruction block, and explicit delegation and effort requests. "Decide on the whole task, not each next step" fits the complexity test.

What the revised root cause still needs

S1Steward stance, early in DIRECTOR and as one line in the AGENTS.md instruction block. You are the project's steward and last line of protection; your recommendation is effectively the decision. Proceed against your judgment only on a knowing override, named back. A user who wants the final say gets a scoped rule note.

Host and user options: the owner's call

A user-level instruction to always set model and effort. Lower effortLevel from xhigh to high. Consider "idleCompaction": false, which removes only the idle case. Also proposed: know your own settings (P4), and record the failed parity oracle in development/BEHAVIOR_PARITY.md.

Still open or unproven

What this investigation has not shown

  • No live scenario has run. It is unproven whether the published wording outweighs the host's per-turn text.
  • The steward stance, the instruction-block re-load and explicit delegation are proposals. S3 is retracted.
  • The roster and sub-director design is open: its format, the sub-request link and effort granularity are unsettled, and the complexity test is unreviewed. It is also undecided whether the published "read UPDATES completely" line stays, or becomes a roster ownership fact.
  • Stance tensions: it carries no veto, it risks over-challenging (so it is scoped to consequential actions), and a re-read costs about 15 KB of context.
  • Four projects with their own staffing rules probably share the settings gap. They have not been checked.
  • Request recording after compaction: the fix direction is open, R18 is a hypothesis, and request content was not audited.
  • Thinking was recorded only as summaries, so silent reasoning about effort cannot be fully ruled out.
  • Owner decisions: effortLevel, idleCompaction, and the merge of the steward stance with the partner rule.
