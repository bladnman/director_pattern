Should personal work live apart from the open-source repo?

Director Pattern is published from one public repository. The same checkout also holds the maintainer's personal and exploratory work. Should that work live somewhere else, the way Tinka keeps a private dev workspace beside its public repo?

Where things are now

Director Pattern today

Private, but backed up nowhere

FactThe local Director installation and preserved snapshots are git-ignored inside the public checkout. A clone does not restore them, and a lost disk loses them.

Personal, but public

FactSome personal material is tracked publicly: the research boards, the local Tinka setup, and this checkout's own Director block in AGENTS.md.

The boundary runs through one folder, drawn by ignore rules. That keeps the private side out of Git, and also out of every backup.

Options considered

A private copy, or a sibling?

(A) Private dev copy
  • Duplicate the folder into a private dev repo.
  • Add a rule: "push changes to public".
  • Two copies of the source.
  • Agents forget the rule, especially after a compaction. The copies drift.
(B) Sibling workspace
  • A private workspace beside the public repo, like Tinka.
  • One copy of the source, edited in place.
  • Nothing to sync, so nothing to forget.
  • Private coordination can get a real backup.

Accepted and approved · November 14, 2026

Where things will live

Public repository
  • The method: pattern/, development/, site/, skill/, README.
  • Root AGENTS.md: maintainer rules and the Tinka pointer only.
  • Research boards and the Tinka setup stay.
  • No local Director installation. Ignore rules stay as a guard.
Private sibling workspace
  • The local Director installation and its request store, tracked privately.
  • The preserved originals, local-only.
  • Its own AGENTS.md with the Director block.
  • A check that both repositories are committed and pushed.

Every session starts in the sibling workspace. Public edits are made in place, in the public checkout. There is still one copy of the source.

The migration, step by step

Eleven steps

StepWhat happens
S0Preconditions: plan approved, no other session in the public checkout, public main clean.
S1Create the private sibling workspace.
S2Freeze and back up the local installation, with a checksum manifest.
S3Move the installation and the originals into the sibling workspace.
S4Re-bind the installation there, with a new technical record and the both-repositories check.
S5An independent worker verifies it before any push.
S6Create the private remote, confirm it is private, push.
S7Clean the public checkout on a branch and update the tests.
S8Record a dated supersession in the rationale, with independent review.
S9Merge and push the public cleanup.
S10Live trials in fresh sessions in both places; record the results.
