Why a Director never proposes a role, and lets specialist calls drift to itself

An adopting video project shipped a video and held a retro. It raised three problems with how the Director worked. All three are one kind of mistake.

Diagnosis accepted Implemented as starter 3.4 Independent review accepted Static tests pass (269) Maintainer acceptance, publication and installation pending · no live trials
Problem 1

No role is ever proposed for work that keeps coming back.

Problem 2

A call that a maintained specialist role owns drifts to the generalist Director.

Problem 3

Every recorded step is treated as fixed. Nobody proposes cutting or merging one.

Labels: Fact seen in a file or record · Hypothesis an inferred cause · Implemented in the starter source, statically tested · Pending not yet done.

The field report, distilled

What happened

  • FactThe run used the starter 3.2 Director file. The project moved to the 3.3 AGENTS.md block after shipping. The rules at issue read the same in both, so the gap carried forward.
  • FactThe installed 3.3 block and cards match the source byte for byte, apart from the project's own marker lines.
  • FactRecurring jobs went to a general analysis worker with a fresh brief every time: after-actions, analysis of the owner's own hand edits, and overnight render and upload chains.
  • FactA maintained packaging role had an explicit rule for titles and thumbnails. The Director judged the owner's own title anyway, wrote eighteen titles in chat, and set a length cap that contradicts the role's rule. The owner corrected it twice. The project's own guidance also contributed: it did not route its packaging check to the packaging role.
  • FactSitReps lapsed during a quiet stretch. The project's Director fixed this itself with a five-minute timer.
  • FactAn answer to a process question was given once in chat and could not be found later.

The project is changing its own guidance and adding a retro role on its own authority. This investigation covers only the upstream pattern.

Generalize first

One kind of mistake

The Director treats the project's maintained structure, its roster and its recorded procedures, as a fixed lookup table. It does not route through that structure or look after it.

Before 3.4, structure was questioned only after a failure
A correction→ An incident→ A bounded review→ A structure proposal
Work that recurred without being corrected→ Nothing
A step that costs more than it returns→ Nothing
Outbound: changing the structure

Problems 1 and 3. No question asked what recurred, or what was not earning its cost.

Inbound: routing through the structure

Problem 2. The Director's direct-answer exception had no carve-out for a judgment that a roster role owns.

HypothesisThe diagnosis is the Director's own. That the missing trigger caused the behavior is inferred, not observed.

Problem 3 · missing

Recorded steps are treated as fixed

  • The partner rule pushes toward the established mechanism. Nothing said one of its steps could itself be questioned.
  • Reassessment was triggered only by a mismatch or a repeated failure.
  • Closure moved stale claims to evidence, not steps.

Same root as problem 1: structure was questioned only after a failure.

Smaller findings

Quiet-stretch SitReps

The rule existed but was incomplete. A per-turn check needs a turn, and nothing arrives during a quiet stretch. The supervision card already said waits must leave the cadence usable; 3.4 spells out how.

Findings lost in chat

Mostly the project's choice. The pattern part is one clause: keep look-back answers where the project keeps findings the user reads back.

A named model out of capacity

Also the project's choice. The starter names no model, and recording the effective settings is already required. No pattern change.

Cards and the project section

Knowledge card: "Look back on the work"

At closure and at any retro, ask two questions about the work this conversation held, not about all history:

  1. What did we brief more than once, or rebuild from scratch, that no roster role holds?
  2. Which steps, records or rules cost more than they returned, or could merge?

Each supported answer goes to the user as one ❓. Steps that come from a user rule, an authority gate or a required independent review are kept. One repeat is a candidate, not a department. Propose nothing when nothing recurred and every step earned its cost.

The other cards
  • Staffing: the role-domain sentence, plus "Accepting a return against the whole request stays yours." A retro or look-back brief carries the two questions.
  • Requests: answer discussion directly, except a call in a roster role's domain, which goes to that role, even in chat. A step not earning its cost is raised as a ❓ cut or merge once; until the user chooses, the step is kept. Repeats go to the look-back.
  • Supervision: arm a supported background timer or wait that leaves you available. If the host has none, say so once and send each due SitRep at your next turn.
  • Project section (new installs): if the roster moves to a file, keep one line per role's domain in the section.

How installed projects receive it

3.4 is the first change to the block since the move to AGENTS.md. Until a 3.3 project updates, its check reports that the block differs from its recorded baseline.

New step: "Update an installed block"
  1. Back up the hook source, the managed files and the receipt.
  2. Replace everything from the opening marker line through the closing one with the incoming block, byte for byte. Leave all other text alone, including the project section; only its --receipt paths change. If the installed block was edited, move that wording into the section first.
  3. Write a receipt with the new card and file baselines and the new block hash. Point the check and update-check commands at it.
  4. Run the check as the section states it. It must report evidence_matched and director_block: unchanged.
Corrected since the first draft
  • Custom markers. A project with its own marker pair puts its two lines in place of the block's own first and last lines. The 3.3 move's step said to keep them around the incoming text, which would have left two marker pairs, and the check accepts exactly one. That step is corrected too.
  • Receipt order. The receipt may now precede the fresh host trials for every block update, not only for the move. Those trials are recorded as pending, and the update is not called adopted until they pass.
  • Scripted test. A 3.3 installation is updated to 3.4, with and without custom markers. The section and a role file are unchanged apart from the receipt paths, and the check passes.

Live cases (none run yet)
  1. Recurrence: after the same analysis is briefed from scratch three times, a retro brings one ❓ role proposal. Nothing is registered before the user accepts.
  2. Domain: asked in chat about a title, with a packaging role on the roster, the Director captures the question and briefs that role. With no matching role, it answers directly.
  3. Cut: a step that cannot change a small request's result is raised as one ❓ and still runs until the user chooses.
  4. Quiet stretch: two long-running workers and a silent user still get a SitRep about every five minutes.
  5. No ceremony: when nothing recurred and every step earned its cost, there is no proposal.

Known-bad cases come from the field run: no role proposed, two packaging corrections, and SitReps lapsing in silence.

Status and remaining risk

  • Accepted risk: the look-back lives on a card, and a card is read only if the Director opens it at its trigger. That trigger was already unproven, and one earlier session skipped the card after a compaction. Naming a retro in the trigger, and putting the questions in every retro brief, narrows the gap without closing it. A live retro is the test.
  • The evidence is one project and one run, on 3.2 text that reads the same as 3.3 here. It is not an observation of 3.3 or 3.4.
  • "A roster role's domain" is judged from roster rows, and a row can be vague or stale. A remark that needed no specialist may still be routed.
  • Installed projects keep their adopted text until they take their own reviewed update. This repository's own installation still holds the 3.3 block.
