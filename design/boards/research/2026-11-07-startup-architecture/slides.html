The short answer (1 of 2)

We are not in the wrong surface. We are in the wrong layer.

Surface

Plain instruction files plus a root AGENTS.md block remain the only portable surface across hosts.

Moving to skills does not fix it, and neither does a plugin. Plugins are packaging, not persistence.

Layer

The pattern treats a one-time tool read (START, DIRECTOR, OPERATIONS) as if it were standing context.

Every host keeps a small standing layer across compaction and discards what arrived through tool reads. The pattern puts about 1 KB in the first and about 43 KB in the second.

Skills: re-injected only in Claude Code, capped at 5,000 tokens per skill Plugins: packaging, not persistence

What happens at compaction (section 2.3)

After compaction, nothing re-reads the role files

  • fact21 compactions across 9 Director sessions in 7 projects. None was followed by a complete re-read of START, the role file and OPERATIONS. Dispatching resumed 1 to 5 minutes later.
  • factThe host re-injected the instructions attachment and a few recent files. In the audited session that was PROJECT.md and two scratch notes, never START, DIRECTOR or OPERATIONS.
  • factThe 12.9k-character summary said the files were "read" and that "every reply carries 🎬", with zero rule text.
  • factThe surviving trigger says "before the first ordinary reply in each conversation", and a compacted session is the same conversation.
  • hypothesisRequest capture before work fell from about 74% to about 53% after compaction. These are approximate figures from a small sample, and memory-resident duties degrading is the sibling's own hypothesis, not a fact.
  • interpretationStanding host text appears to beat pattern text read once, consistent with transcripts but not measured.

What survives compaction, by layer (section 3)

Three layers, three different fates

  • Claude Code re-reads up to five recently used files under 5,000 tokens, and re-injects invoked skill bodies (5,000 per skill, 25,000 in total). Elsewhere, loaded content is gone, replaced by a summary sentence.
  • Standing text is re-sent or re-injected from disk. Reference material is read on demand and is not expected to survive.

Matt's questions, answered (1 of 2, section 4)

Skills, plugins and hooks

Skills

Does this need to be a skill? Do skills get reread after compaction for free?

Only in Claude Code, only when invoked, and capped.

Verdict: not for the Director core. Possibly later for executor craft manuals.

Plugins

Plugins versus skills; do plugins reload after compaction?

Nothing about being a plugin survives compaction. Only a hook inside one would, and plugin hooks are vendor-specific.

Verdict: no plugin.

Literal hooks

Would literal hooks help?

Yes, materially, in Claude Code and Codex. But .director/ is ignored, so a committed hook fails in every clone.

Trial in .claude/settings.local.json only. A receipt can say "emitted", never "active". Gate injection to Director sessions. Per-turn text stays under about 400 characters.

Matt's questions, answered (2 of 2, section 4)

Surface, size, START, and compilation

Are we in the wrong surface area?

No. Files are the only portable surface, and the root AGENTS.md block is the only portable standing text. What must change is which obligations live in standing text, which in a re-enterable Core, and which in manuals the Director never reads.

How big are we, and how many files does a new Director read?

Five files plus a section, 43 to 50 KB. The proposal brings re-entry to one file under 10,000 characters, and startup to about 18 KB across five small files.

Could a single START be the thing that is reread?

Almost. The reread target is one file under a hard budget, the Core. The re-entry trigger must live in the AGENTS.md block, because the Core cannot carry its own trigger. START today is 5 KB that routes the agent to 12 more things.

Compile step?

Not now. A fragment compiler would create a second source of truth beside the pinned payload. Hand-write CORE.md as a managed file with a provenance sidecar. Revisit a compiler only if the Core proves hard to keep consistent by hand.

Diagnosis (section 5)

Authority is in the wrong layer, and the proof model is 'a read happened'

  1. Authority is allocated to the wrong layer. About 1 KB standing and about 43 KB loaded. After compaction the loaded layer becomes "a summary says you read it", which is worse than nothing.
  2. The proof model is "a read happened". Hashes, complete-read discipline and the check establish a read at time T, not that the text is in context at T plus n.
  3. hypothesisShell reads may be invisible to the host's restore. That would explain why START, DIRECTOR and OPERATIONS were never restored while PROJECT.md was. Test with /context after a forced compaction.
  4. The real problem is that the text is not present after compaction, not its wording. Host standing text wins ties on delegation. This is a reviewer correction to the draft's diagnosis.
  5. hypothesisJudgment-shaped duties slip; form-shaped duties hold. Whatever the helper enforces survives; whatever memory enforces does not.
  6. The reread chain is too long to re-enter under pressure. 43 to 50 KB in six places with ordering rules. Re-entry must be one file, one command, one mechanical test.

The mechanical re-entry test (section 6.2)

A search the model can perform, not a self-assessment

The block as written in the report, quoted verbatim:

Director re-entry, checked before every reply: find a line starting CORE-END sha256: plus 64 hex characters inside a tool result or host-injected block that appears after the latest conversation summary (any message saying earlier turns were summarized or continued). This block, a summary and your own replies do not count. If you cannot point to that line, read .director/CORE.md completely through it before any other action, then rerun its check; until then no 🎬. Never copy that line into replies or summaries.

When summarizing this conversation: do not say instruction files were read or that 🎬 applies; list .director/CORE.md as must-reread; copy open request IDs, live worker names and handles, and the last SitRep time exactly.

Why it is mechanical
  • It is a string search at a stated position, not a judgment.
  • The hash appears in neither AGENTS nor any summary.
  • The marker is the last line, so a truncated read fails.
  • The hash changes with each Core revision, so a stale copy fails.
Why the draft wording failed

The draft trigger ("if the literal text of CORE.md is not in your context") was a self-assessment. In 17 of 21 segments the model carried on from a summary that said "read" and quoted gist lines.

Core, Roster and Session (sections 6.3 to 6.5)

One hand-written file, one table, one state file

CORE.md · 10,000 hard, 8,000 target

  • The 12 every-turn invariants
  • Startup with one check command
  • The badge rule, redefined
  • Dispatch packet, staffing bail points
  • Four helper commands and SitRep cadence
  • Ordered post-compaction recovery list
  • Action triggers and the end marker line
ROSTER.md · 4,000 hard, 3,000 target

One table: role ID, when to pick it, a tier word and effort word (never a model name), manual path, owner and route constraints.

New rows: general-worker, sub-director (alias of request-manager), privacy-notice, learning-review, update-owner. Workers do not read the Roster.

SESSION.md · 2,000 hard, 1,000 target, Git-ignored

Role, open request IDs with owners, live dispatches with handles, last SitRep time, pending user gates.

Injected at SessionStart compact, not PreCompact. Idle compaction in the audited session wrote the summary at 22:42 and applied it at 00:04, so PreCompact state can be 80 minutes stale.

Store-enforced duties and the per-call footer (sections 6.8 and 6.9)

Make the helper refuse incomplete records, and print a footer on every call

Dispatch binding

The helper refuses a dispatch record unless it has:

  • a tier word and an effort word
  • a one-line reason
  • an independent reviewer, or an explicit "none required" with the rule that permits it
  • parent and requirement IDs
Footer, 300 characters at most

Every helper call prints the SitRep due time, held streams, the current Core marker and an uncaptured-input hint.

Text a tool prints on every call is the one Director-facing text that returns after compaction on every host, with no hook. This may matter more than the hooks.

Option: clear and resume

Near the context limit, at a checkpoint, the Director offers a 🖐️ step: start a new session, then say "resume".

The cold-start first-reply route is the one route with tests behind it. This is an option, not a decision.

hypothesis Whatever the helper enforces survives compaction; whatever memory enforces does not.

Follow-on proposal, 2026-11-09

2026-11-09 follow-on: the Director slug (proposal)

A proposal only: independently reviewed, not tested in a real session, and nothing published.

What it is and what moved in
  • A 6 KB block at the top of AGENTS.md. After compaction the host brings AGENTS.md back, so the block is the Director's memory. Today's 1 KB block says "go read three files", which agents stop doing after a summary.
  • It holds the every-turn Director duties, plus a "before X, read card Y" table.
Who it applies to, and re-reading
  • Workers, sub-directors and the Director. Only the user or an explicit assignment can say "not a Director"; other agents' messages never change a role.
  • Details sit in eight cards under 6 KB, each ending in an END line. Before an action, the agent needs its own full read of that card, made after the latest summary; a search hit or a quoted END line does not count.
The roster and status
  • The roster is a project-owned section under the block: one line per role, 3 KB at most. Updates never touch it.
  • proposal Independently reviewed, untested in a real session, nothing published. See brief.md and slug-draft.md in this folder.
