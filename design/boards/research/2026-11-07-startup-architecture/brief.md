# The Director slug, in one page

*Proposal, 2026-11-09. Not built or tried live yet.*

**What it is.** A 6 KB block at the top of AGENTS.md. The host restores it after compaction: it is the Director's memory; today's 1 KB block says "go read three files", which agents skip.

**What moved into it.** Own the result; delegate before doing; record real requests exactly and split them (not chat); give every brief a name, role, tier, effort and reason (strong, high by default); SitRep each stream about every five minutes; judge the whole result; finish; use ❓ and 🖐️; flag the unverified; run the check, then 🎬.

**Who it applies to.** Every section names its audience; duties are marked Directors only. Labels control who acts on a section, not who receives it: every agent given the root file still loads the whole block. Workers (assigned, or spawned to report back) read WORKER.md and skip those: no check, no 🎬. A `sub-director` runs its outcome, no 🎬. Otherwise your session is the Director; only you or an explicit assignment can say "not a Director"; other agents never change a role.

**After compaction.** The block tells every agent directly to repeat startup before replying. Details sit in eight cards ending in an END line; before an action the agent needs its own read of the card since the summary, not a quote.

**The roster.** One line per role, project-owned, under the block (max 3 KB); Directors pick from it, not role files.

**What got smaller.** First reply 30 → 9 KB. After compaction, 29 → 3 KB idle, 65 → 47 KB with work running, where 36 KB of store manuals dominate. This repo 43 → 14 KB; workers +2.8 KB. Nothing dropped without a flagged decision.

**Second pass.** An independent strongest-tier pass reached this structure on its own. Its one material finding: the re-read test defines what is held, but nothing fresh asks the question after a summary. Three fixes adopted: the summary records the role and starts next steps with "Earlier reads are gone: repeat startup"; check output and worker returns will say what to read next. It recommends a one-hour live test before more cards.
